About this guide

A practical guide to working with AI agents so their output can be checked before it ships.

What it is

The guide is a short, single page covering the ideas that matter most when you start working with coding agents: the harness, the agent loop, human gates, context, and how a team setup fits together. The articles go deeper on each of those, with worked examples.

It is not a tool review or a list of prompts. Tools and models change every few months. The ideas here have held up across several generations of both.

Who it is for

  • Developers who use a coding agent every day and want it to do more of the work without more babysitting.
  • Team leads deciding how agents should fit into review, testing and deployment.
  • People who do not write code but use chat apps and agents for drafting, research and office work. Most of the ideas apply unchanged: clear criteria, written-down knowledge, and a person approving anything that matters.

The perspective

It is written by a practitioner who builds and runs agent harnesses every day, for software and for office work. That means writing the instructions, skills and checks, watching where agents go wrong, and fixing the setup rather than the output.

So the advice leans practical. It favours small, checkable steps over big promises, and it is opinionated where experience has been consistent. Where an idea comes from someone else's public writing, it is credited in the further reading list.

The examples use made-up names. Fernhill Supply is not a real shop, and Sam is not a real developer.

How to use it

  1. Read the guide once, top to bottom. It takes about twenty minutes.
  2. Try the first exercise on a real repo. One gated change teaches more than any amount of reading.
  3. Go to the article for whichever part felt weakest in your own setup.
  4. Come back when something goes wrong. When an agent does something dumb, ask what the harness gave it, and look up that part.

If something is wrong, unclear or out of date, see the contact page.
